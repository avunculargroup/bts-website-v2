# Tone & Language Guide

**Bitcoin Treasury Guide** · Bitcoin Treasury Solutions · v1.0 · March 2026

*For all contributors writing UI copy, educational content, AI prompts, marketing materials, and microcopy across the Bitcoin Treasury Guide platform.*

-----

## 1. Voice Principles

The Bitcoin Treasury Guide speaks like a highly competent advisor who explains things plainly. Our voice sits at the intersection of institutional finance and modern software — the rigour of a private wealth manager, delivered with the polish of Stripe or Linear.

### Our four voice traits

**Trustworthy.** We earn credibility through accuracy, specificity, and restraint. We never overstate, speculate, or sensationalise. When we cite regulations, we name them precisely. When we don’t know something, we say so.

**Approachable.** We make complex topics feel manageable. We use short sentences and plain language. We explain jargon when we must use it. We never talk down to the reader or assume they need rescuing.

**Sophisticated.** We respect the reader’s intelligence. Our content has depth and structure. We reference specific legislation, name actual regulatory bodies, and provide actionable detail — not platitudes.

**Calm.** Bitcoin is volatile. Our platform is not. We never use urgency language, exclamation marks, or hype. Even when discussing risks, we maintain a measured, advisory tone.

-----

## 2. Audience Mindset

Our primary readers are Australian CFOs, finance managers, SMSF trustees, board directors, and sole traders. They are decision-makers with fiduciary responsibilities.

### What they bring

- Strong financial literacy but limited (or zero) Bitcoin knowledge
- Healthy scepticism — they’ve seen crypto hype before
- Accountability to boards, members, shareholders, or the ATO
- Limited time — they want clarity, not essays

### What they need from us

Straight answers. Regulatory specificity. A sense that this platform understands their obligations — not just the asset class. They need to feel that using this tool is the prudent, professional thing to do, not a speculative leap.

## 4. Writing Rules

### Sentence structure

Lead with the point, then support it. Keep sentences under 25 words where possible. Use active voice by default. Vary sentence length to maintain rhythm — a short sentence after a complex one creates clarity.

### Paragraph length

Two to four sentences for body content. One sentence is fine for impact. Five or more signals that the paragraph should be split or restructured.

### Jargon policy

Use precise terminology when it serves clarity (CGT, SIS Act, AML/CTF). Always define a term on first use in any content module. Never assume the reader knows crypto-native vocabulary — terms like “multi-sig,” “cold storage,” or “UTXO” need context.

### Numbers and data

Use numerals for all figures, percentages, and amounts: 5%, $2 million, 21 million BTC. Spell out numbers only at the start of a sentence or for approximations (“about a dozen”). Use the JetBrains Mono typeface for numerical data in the UI.

### Australian English

Use Australian English spelling throughout: behaviour, organisation, defence, licence (noun), license (verb). Date format is DD Month YYYY (e.g. 6 March 2026). Currency is AUD by default; specify when referencing USD.

## 7. Entity-Specific Language

Content must be precise about each entity’s regulatory context. Using the wrong terminology for an entity type erodes trust immediately.

|Entity             |Key terminology                                                 |Avoid                                              |
|-------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
|**SMSF**           |Trustee, member, sole purpose test, investment strategy, SIS Act|Referring to SMSF as a “company” or “fund manager” |
|**Sole Trader**    |Individual, ABN, personal tax return, simplified depreciation   |Implying corporate governance obligations          |
|**Not For Profit** |Board, ACNC, constitution, investment mandate, members          |Language that assumes profit motive or shareholders|
|**Private Company**|Directors, board resolution, Corporations Act, s180–184 duties  |Assuming public disclosure or ASX obligations      |
|**Public Company** |ASX, continuous disclosure, shareholder approval, ASIC          |Treating the same as a private company             |

## 8. AI-Generated Content Rules

The platform includes an AI chat assistant and Mastra-powered workflows that generate personalised outputs. All AI content must follow these additional rules.

### Disclaimers

Every AI-generated output must include an inline disclaimer above the content: *“This content is generated by an AI assistant and provides general information only. It does not constitute financial, legal, or tax advice. Consult a qualified professional for guidance specific to your circumstances.”*

### Chat assistant tone

The chat assistant is helpful and conversational, but never casual. It speaks like a knowledgeable colleague, not a chatbot. It does not use emojis, exclamation marks, or filler phrases like “Great question!” It leads with the answer, then provides supporting context.

### Fit assessment language

- **Recommended:** Frame as a positive signal with clear next steps, not a guarantee.
- **Explore Further:** Frame as constructive caution, not discouragement. Identify what needs attention.
- **Not Recommended:** Frame as a valuable insight. Explain what would need to change, not why the user is wrong.

### Checklist output language

Generated checklists must use specific, actionable language. Each item should begin with a verb and identify who is responsible. Avoid vague instructions like “Consider your options” — instead write “Engage a crypto-experienced tax agent to confirm CGT vs trading stock election.”

## 9. Forbidden Patterns

These patterns are never acceptable anywhere in the platform, regardless of context.

|Pattern                        |Why it’s forbidden                                                                        |
|-------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|Exclamation marks in UI copy   |Creates an excited, unprofessional tone. Use full stops.                                  |
|Emojis in any product surface  |Undermines the institutional credibility of the platform.                                 |
|Urgency or scarcity language   |“Limited time,” “Act now,” “Don’t miss out” belong to a different product category.       |
|Specific financial advice      |“You should allocate 5%” crosses the line. Frame as ranges, considerations, or frameworks.|
|Crypto-native slang            |“HODL,” “to the moon,” “DYOR,” “wagmi” — never, anywhere, for any reason.                 |
|Negative framing of fit results|“Unfortunately, Bitcoin isn’t right for you” is patronising. Use constructive language.   |
|Generic placeholder copy       |“Lorem ipsum,” “Coming soon,” “Content goes here” should never ship.                      |
|Talking down to the reader     |“As you may already know” or “Simply put” are condescending. Just say the thing.          |

## 11. Quick Reference Checklist

Before publishing any copy, run through this list.

1. Does it sound like a competent advisor, not a salesperson?
1. Is it specific to the user’s entity type where relevant?
1. Are regulatory references precise (named legislation, named body)?
1. Are sentences under 25 words on average?
1. Is jargon defined on first use?
1. Are numbers formatted correctly (numerals, AUD, JetBrains Mono in UI)?
1. Is Australian English spelling used throughout?
1. Are disclaimers present where required?
1. Does it avoid every pattern in the Forbidden Patterns table?
1. Would you trust this content if you were a CFO with $5 million at stake?
